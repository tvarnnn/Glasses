## Run metadata

- Date: 2026-08-20
- Dataset: EPIC-KITCHENS-100, sequence `P01_107`, 1920×1080 @ 50 fps,
  119.4 s, 5972 frames, CC BY-NC 4.0. Head-mounted GoPro; real optics,
  real compression, undirected natural head motion during ordinary
  activity.
- Sampling: every 3rd frame (stride 3) → **16.67 fps effective**,
  approximating the platform's ~15 fps target (`03-ROADMAP.md` V0.7).
  Sampling at native 50 fps would understate flicker and overstate
  trackability by making consecutive frames unnaturally similar.
- Input resampling: frames resized to **896×504**, aspect preserved.
  **This matters for Experiment 2 but barely affects Experiment 1** —
  see the note on analysis resolution below.
- Window analysed: first **150 sampled frames** (~9 s of footage).
- Device: **CPU** (`torch 2.13.0+cpu`). Experiment 1 measures output
  *stability*, which is device-independent; the per-frame timing figure
  is therefore **not** a CPU-vs-GPU comparison. See
  `V0.9.1-depth-cv-baseline-report.md` for that.
- Harness-introduced confound: frames are re-encoded to JPEG at quality
  95 before being handed to the experiment, stacking a second lossy
  encode on the dataset's existing H.264. This is **not** the platform's
  adaptive-bitrate regime, which the dataset ruling names as the single
  biggest threat to both experiments' transferability.

### Analysis resolution (Experiment 1)

MiDaS-small's own `small_transform` emits a fixed **128×256** tensor for
any 16:9 input — verified directly: 1920×1080, 1280×720, 640×360 and
896×504 all produce 128×256. So the depth maps analysed below are at
roughly 1/7th the linear resolution of the platform's 504×896 budget, and
the input resize changes little for this experiment beyond anti-aliasing.
Fine-scale flicker is spatially averaged away before measurement; the
figures are therefore a **lower bound** on per-pixel flicker at full
resolution.

# Experiment 1 — `depth_temporal_consistency`

## Success Criteria (per `docs/modules/EXPERIMENTAL-CV.md`)

- **Hypothesis/question:** Is naive per-frame MiDaS-small relative depth
  too unstable frame-to-frame on real continuous motion to support
  anything that accumulates confidence over repeated observation — and
  does a cheap post-hoc smoothing filter meaningfully reduce that
  instability at an acceptable lag cost?
- **Dataset/session:** 150 sampled frames as described above.
- **Baseline:** raw, unsmoothed per-frame output.
- **Resource usage:** 31.31 ms average per frame for the full shipped
  experiment path (decode → preprocess → inference → postprocess), CPU,
  first frame excluded as warm-up.

### Metrics, and what each can and cannot show

- **`mad_mean`** — mean absolute difference between consecutive
  normalized depth maps. The headline flicker measure.
- **`p95_mean`** — 95th-percentile per-pixel change: worst-region flicker.
- **`local_temporal_std_mean`** — per-pixel std over a **5-frame sliding
  window** (~0.3 s), averaged. Deliberately *not* computed over the whole
  150-frame window: across 9 s of continuous head motion the scene content
  changes completely, so a whole-sequence std measures camera movement,
  not estimator stability.
- **`deviation_from_raw`** — mean |smoothed − raw|. Reported as "how much
  the smoother changed the output" and **nothing more**. It is explicitly
  **not** a lag measure: a smoother that removed all noise with zero delay
  would still score high here, because the raw signal it is being compared
  against is itself noisy. It is also not comparable to `mad_mean` (a
  first-difference statistic vs. an instantaneous level difference).
- **`step_response_lag_frames`** — the actual lag figure, measured
  separately against a known synthetic step (0→1), since the depth data
  has no ground truth to measure delay against.

Three normalization strategies are reported rather than one, because
per-frame min-max normalization — the obvious choice — is pathologically
sensitive to single-pixel outliers (one pixel sets the scale for the whole
frame). Agreement across all three is what makes the result trustworthy.

## Measured

**Actual lag, measured against a known step** (frames to reach 63.2% of a
unit step; at 16.67 fps, 1 frame ≈ 60 ms):

| Smoother | Lag (frames) | Lag (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| EMA α=0.5 | 2.0 | ~120 ms |
| EMA α=0.3 | 3.0 | ~180 ms |
| median w=3 | 2.0 | ~120 ms |
| median w=5 | 3.0 | ~180 ms |

**Flicker, all three normalizers** (smoothed frames re-normalized before
measurement, so reductions reflect stabilization rather than amplitude
compression):

| **raw baseline** | **0.07148** | 0.20426 | 0.07485 | — | 0.0 |
| EMA α=0.5 | 0.04596 | 0.12711 | 0.05686 | 35.70% | 0.04215 |
| EMA α=0.3 | 0.03549 | 0.09270 | 0.04628 | **50.35%** | 0.06983 |

| median w=5 | 0.03505 | 0.12914 | 0.05121 | **50.97%** | 0.07956 |

|---|---|---|---|---|

| median w=3 | 0.04485 | 0.16862 | 0.06375 | 41.03% |
| median w=5 | 0.03600 | 0.14171 | 0.05402 | 52.66% |

### median/MAD normalization — the MiDaS-literature family

| Variant | `mad_mean` | `p95_mean` | `local_std` | Reduction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **raw baseline** | **0.06330** | 0.19246 | 0.06750 | — |
| EMA α=0.5 | 0.04782 | 0.13662 | 0.05919 | 24.45% |
| EMA α=0.3 | 0.03644 | 0.09845 | 0.04747 | **42.43%** |

| median w=5 | 0.03868 | 0.14673 | 0.05534 | 38.89% |

## Result

**Flicker is real and substantial, and cheap causal smoothing genuinely
reduces it at a modest, now-quantified lag cost.**

- **The finding is robust to normalization choice.** Raw baseline flicker
  is 0.0633–0.0761 across all three strategies — roughly **6–8% of full
  depth range between consecutive frames**, with worst regions
  (`p95`) moving **19–23%**. Because the outlier-robust percentile and
  median/MAD variants agree with min-max, the measurement is not an
  artifact of a fragile normalization. The literature's claim that
  per-frame MiDaS flickers on video is confirmed on this footage.
- **EMA α=0.3 is the best cheap option tested**, at 42–53% flicker
  reduction depending on normalizer, and it is the cheapest to run
  (O(1) state, one retained frame, versus a median's O(w) frames plus a
  sort). Median w=5 matches it on flicker but costs more lag and more
  memory.
- **The lag cost is ~3 frames (~180 ms) for EMA α=0.3, 2 frames
  (~120 ms) for α=0.5.** This is a real, bounded, measured trade — and it
  is a genuine engineering choice, not a free win, because ~180 ms of
  staleness is material for anything reacting to motion. It is *not*, as
  the first draft of this report wrongly implied, a cost that exceeds the
  benefit; that claim came from misreading a level-difference statistic as
  a lag measure.
- **Smoothing also improves short-window stability**, which is the
  property that matters for accumulating confidence over repeated
  observation: `local_temporal_std` falls from 0.0749 to 0.0463 (−38%)
  under EMA α=0.3 on min-max. Measured over a 5-frame window where scene
  content is roughly stationary, this says the estimate really does settle
  — unlike the first draft's whole-sequence figure, which was dominated by
  the camera moving through a kitchen and could not have shown this either
  way.

**Answering the hypothesis directly:** the cheap fix works well enough to
be worth having. Roughly half the frame-to-frame flicker is removable for
~180 ms of lag and negligible compute, with no new dependency. That does
**not** mean the problem is solved — half the flicker remains, and this is
a lower bound measured on a 128×256 grid from a camera that is not the
platform's. The escalation path (a dedicated temporally-consistent
video-depth model) is neither taken nor ruled out here; per
`01-SYSTEM-ARCHITECTURE.md`'s correctness → instrument → profile →
accelerate discipline, the trigger for reconsidering it would be a
measured requirement this cheap filter cannot meet, on real footage.

# Experiment 2 — `feature_trackability`

## Success Criteria (per `docs/modules/EXPERIMENTAL-CV.md`)

- **Hypothesis/question:** Does ordinary, undirected head-mounted motion
  produce frame pairs with enough shared, geometrically-verified
  structure to make any multi-view geometry worth attempting at all?
- **Dataset/session:** same 150 sampled frames.
- **Baseline:** none needed — this is an existence question about the
  data, not a comparison between approaches.
- **Method note:** verification uses the **fundamental matrix with a
  homography cross-check, never `recoverPose`** — so this experiment needs
  **no camera intrinsics** and is unaffected by the intrinsics gap that
  blocks Experiment 3.

### On comparing homography and fundamental inliers

Raw H-vs-F inlier counts are **not** directly comparable: the fundamental
residual is point-to-epipolar-*line* (a 1-D constraint) while the
homography residual is point-to-*point* (2-D), so F is the strictly weaker
test and H ≤ F almost always, for any scene geometry. This report
therefore uses **ORB-SLAM's model-selection statistic
`R_H = S_H / (S_H + S_F)` with its established 0.45 threshold**, above
which the homography model is selected — indicating near-pure rotation or
a dominant plane, i.e. geometry that is degenerate for triangulation.

## Measured

ORB (`nfeatures=1000`): **950.83 keypoints/frame mean** (median 996, min
229, max 1000). Per-frame cost **4.67 ms** excluding the warm-up frame
(median 4.68 ms), covering **resize + grayscale + `detectAndCompute`**.

| Frame gap | Matches | Verified inliers (mean/median) | Inlier ratio | **R_H (mean/median)** | **Rotation-dominant pairs** | Pairs ≥30 inliers | Match cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **k=1** | 431.0 | **386.5 / 384** | 0.885 | **0.451 / 0.455** | **53.69%** | **100.0%** | 1.88 ms |
| k=2 | 303.6 | 261.3 / 252 | 0.836 | 0.433 / 0.443 | 41.22% | **100.0%** | 2.39 ms |
| k=5 | 152.3 | 123.0 / 97 | 0.758 | 0.405 / 0.410 | 12.41% | 72.41% | 4.23 ms |
| k=10 | 67.2 | 51.2 / 24 | 0.645 | 0.393 / 0.394 | 11.72% | 47.14% | 5.47 ms |

## Result

**Clear positive on the headline question, with a real and now
properly-quantified structural caveat.**

- **Trackable structure is abundant at the rates the platform runs.** At
  k=1, **100% of 149 frame pairs** cleared 30 verified inliers, averaging
  **386 inliers at an 0.885 inlier ratio**. The research pass's central
  worry — that casual wearable motion might be a fundamentally harder
  regime than the deliberate/robot-mounted motion in standard VO
  benchmarks, with matches collapsing under real head motion — **is not
  supported by this footage.** That meaningfully de-risks the World
  Builder direction.
- **Cost is comfortably within budget, on a like-for-like basis.**
  Detection (4.67 ms, now including resize and grayscale so it is
  measured on the same footing as the depth figure) plus matching
  (1.88 ms at k=1) is **~6.6 ms/frame** on CPU, no GPU and no model
  download. Note this is *additive* to the depth experiment's 31.31 ms if
  a future pipeline runs both, not absorbed into it.
- **Shared structure decays fast with frame gap**, and the median decays
  faster than the mean (k=10: mean 51.2 but median 24 — a minority of
  well-textured pairs holds the mean up while the typical pair has
  degraded). Any future keyframe policy should key off measured match
  counts, not a fixed frame interval.
- **⚠ Caveat — over half of consecutive-frame pairs are
  rotation-dominant.** At k=1, `R_H` averages **0.451** and **53.69% of
  pairs exceed ORB-SLAM's 0.45 threshold**, meaning a homography explains
  them at least as well as epipolar geometry — the signature of near-pure
  rotation or a dominant plane, which is **degenerate for
  triangulation** even though match counts look excellent. The effect
  falls off sharply with baseline: 41.22% at k=2, **12.41% at k=5**,
  11.72% at k=10.
- **This produces a concrete, measured tension for Experiment 3**, which
  is the most useful thing this experiment yields beyond the positive
  result: *consecutive frames match superbly but over half carry too
  little parallax to triangulate, while k=5 pairs have good parallax
  (12% degenerate) but only 72% clear the inlier floor and the typical
  pair has fallen to 97 inliers.* Whatever pose approach is eventually
  tried will have to pick its baseline against that curve rather than
  defaulting to consecutive frames.

## Conclusions

1. **Both experiments answered their question with measured numbers.**
   Depth flicker on real motion is confirmed, quantified, and roughly
   halved by a cheap causal filter costing ~180 ms of lag. Trackable
   structure under undirected head-mounted motion is abundant and cheap.
2. **Most important new fact:** passive, undirected wearable motion does
   **not** appear to be the trackability dead-end flagged as the research
   pass's biggest open risk — on this footage.
3. **Most important new caveat:** parallax, not match count, is the
   binding constraint at short baselines — 53.7% of consecutive-frame
   pairs are rotation-dominant by ORB-SLAM's own criterion, dropping to
   ~12% by k=5. This is now measured against an established threshold
   rather than asserted from an invalid ratio.
4. **Nothing here validates the platform's own camera.** The acceptance
   gate stands.

## What was NOT done (and why)

- **Experiment 3 (`monocular_pose_feasibility`) — not started.** Blocked
  on DAT camera intrinsics, which exist nowhere in this repository and
  require either `search_dat_docs` (Mac-side session) or empirical
  calibration. No substitute intrinsics were invented — a guessed focal
  length would produce a plausible-looking trajectory with no validity.
- **Experiment 4 (`depth_scale_fusion`) — not started.** Depends on
  Experiment 3 or on physically-measured reference distances.
- **No smoothing stage was added to the shipped module.** Choosing a
  production smoothing strategy on dataset-only evidence would violate the
  acceptance gate.
- **No new model or acceleration dependency was adopted.** No measured
  trigger was produced (see
  `docs/superpowers/research/2026-08-20-gpu-nvidia-roadmap.md`).

## Corrections from the first draft

A code review found three metrics that could not bear the conclusions
drawn from them. All were fixed and both experiments re-run; the
superseded figures are recorded here rather than silently replaced.

| Issue | First draft | Corrected |
|---|---|---|
| **`lag_from_raw` presented as lag.** Mean \|smoothed − raw\| is large whenever a smoother removes noise, even at zero delay; and the draft called the raw MiDaS output "the current true frame", treating inference as ground truth (Core Principle 2 violation, in a report that cites Core Principle 2). | "EMA α=0.3 … introduces 0.0694 lag, larger than the 0.0416 of flicker it removes"; "the trade is close to one-for-one, which is the finding that actually matters" | Renamed `deviation_from_raw` and explicitly disclaimed as not-lag. Real lag measured against a known step: **3 frames / ~180 ms** for EMA α=0.3. The one-for-one trade claim was **wrong and is withdrawn.** |
| **Flicker reduction inflated by amplitude compression.** Smoothed frames were not re-normalized, so their shrunken dynamic range trivially produced smaller absolute differences. | EMA α=0.3: **58.14%** reduction | Smoothed frames re-normalized before measurement: **50.35%** (min-max). ~8 points of the original figure was compression, not stabilization. |
| **Whole-window `temporal_std` misread.** Computed over all 150 frames (~9 s of continuous motion through a changing scene), it measured camera movement, not estimator stability — so the draft's conclusion drawn from it was unsupportable in either direction. | "temporal_std barely improves … the underlying estimate genuinely moves; it is not just noisy around a steady mean" | Recomputed over a 5-frame sliding window: **0.0749 → 0.0463 (−38%)** under EMA α=0.3. The draft's conclusion is **reversed** — the estimate does settle at short timescales. |
| **Invalid H-vs-F comparison.** The draft compared homography and fundamental inlier counts directly at the same 3 px threshold, but the two residuals are 2-D and 1-D respectively, so H ≤ F holds for any geometry and the ratio proves nothing. | "homography inliers are 86% of fundamental inliers … suggests degeneracy" | ORB-SLAM's `R_H = S_H/(S_H+S_F)` with its 0.45 threshold: **53.69% of k=1 pairs are rotation-dominant.** The caveat survives — better supported, and now with a decay curve across k. |
| **Incomparable cost figures / inconsistent warm-up.** Experiment 2 timed only `detectAndCompute` while Experiment 1's figure included decode and preprocess; Experiment 1 silently excluded its warm-up frame while Experiment 2 did not. | "5.91 ms … ~7.8 ms/frame … comfortably within the ~20–30 ms envelope" | Timer widened to resize + grayscale + detect; warm-up excluded in both: **4.67 ms + 1.88 ms = ~6.6 ms**, and stated as *additive* to depth's cost rather than fitting inside it. |
