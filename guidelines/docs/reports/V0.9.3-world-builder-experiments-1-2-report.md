# Experiment 1 — `depth_temporal_consistency`

## Success Criteria (per `docs/modules/EXPERIMENTAL-CV.md`)

- **Hypothesis/question:** Is naive per-frame MiDaS-small relative depth
  too unstable frame-to-frame on real continuous motion to support
  anything that accumulates confidence over repeated observation — and
  does a cheap post-hoc smoothing filter meaningfully reduce that
  instability without unacceptable lag?
- **Dataset/session:** 150 sampled frames as described in Run metadata.
- **Metrics:** on per-frame min-max-normalized depth maps (0–1 units):
  `mad_mean` (mean absolute difference between consecutive frames — the
  headline flicker measure), `mad_max`, `p95_mean` (95th-percentile
  per-pixel change, i.e. worst-region flicker), `temporal_std_mean`
  (per-pixel std over time, averaged). Plus `lag_from_raw`: mean absolute
  deviation of the smoothed output from the raw output, quantifying the
  responsiveness cost of smoothing.
- **Baseline:** raw, unsmoothed per-frame output.
- **Resource usage:** 29.68 ms average per frame for the full shipped
  experiment path (decode → preprocess → inference → postprocess), CPU.

### Why normalization matters to reading these numbers

MiDaS output is relative inverse depth with an arbitrary per-frame
scale/offset. Comparing raw consecutive frames would mostly measure that
arbitrary drift, not structural flicker, so each frame is min-max
normalized first. This is the standard approach, but it has a real
consequence worth stating: a global scale shift between frames still
shows up as flicker, so part of the measured instability is
normalization-induced rather than structural.

## Measured

Depth map shape: 128×256 (MiDaS-small's native output grid).

| Variant | `mad_mean` | `mad_max` | `p95_mean` | `temporal_std_mean` | Flicker reduction | `lag_from_raw` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **raw baseline** | **0.07148** | 0.32935 | 0.20426 | 0.20500 | — | 0.0 |
| EMA α=0.5 | 0.04158 | 0.15036 | 0.11491 | 0.18520 | **41.83%** | 0.04131 |
| EMA α=0.3 | 0.02992 | 0.08217 | 0.07765 | 0.16738 | **58.14%** | 0.06936 |
| median w=3 | 0.04112 | 0.21813 | 0.14908 | 0.19740 | **42.47%** | 0.05115 |
| median w=5 | 0.03244 | 0.15890 | 0.12532 | 0.19085 | **54.62%** | 0.07779 |

## Result

**Flicker is real and substantial, and cheap causal smoothing measurably
reduces it — but the trade is close to one-for-one, which is the finding
that actually matters.**

- Raw consecutive frames differ by **0.0715 in normalized depth units on
  average** — about **7% of the full depth range per frame**, with worst
  frames reaching **0.329 (33%)**. The literature's claim that per-frame
  MiDaS flickers on video is confirmed on this footage; it is not an
  assumption this platform needs to keep taking on faith.
- Worst-region flicker is worse than the average suggests: the
  95th-percentile per-pixel change is **0.204**, i.e. the noisiest 5% of
  the map moves ~20% of full range between consecutive frames.
- **EMA α=0.3 is the strongest of the cheap options tested** (58.1%
  flicker reduction), slightly ahead of median w=5 (54.6%) at *lower* lag
  (0.069 vs 0.078). EMA is also cheaper (O(1) state, one frame retained)
  than a trailing median (O(w) frames retained plus a sort).
- **The cost side is not negligible.** EMA α=0.3 removes 0.0416 of
  flicker (0.0715 → 0.0299) while introducing 0.0694 of deviation from
  the current true frame. In these units the lag introduced is *larger
  than the flicker removed*. Smoothing is not a free win — it trades a
  fast-moving wrong estimate for a slow-moving stale one.
- **`temporal_std_mean` barely improves** across every variant (0.205 →
  0.167 at best, ~18%) even where `mad_mean` improves by 58%. This is the
  most diagnostically useful number in the table: it says the smoothing
  is suppressing high-frequency frame-to-frame *jitter* but is **not**
  making the depth map settle onto a stable value over the window. The
  underlying estimate genuinely moves; it is not just noisy around a
  steady mean.

**Answering the hypothesis directly:** the cheap fix (option 2 in the
research doc) is *partially* sufficient. It clearly suppresses jitter, so
it is worth having. It does **not** deliver a stable accumulated estimate,
so the research doc's escalation path — a dedicated
temporally-consistent video-depth model (Video-Depth-Anything class) —
now has a **measured** reason to be reconsidered later, rather than the
speculative one it had before. Per `01-SYSTEM-ARCHITECTURE.md`'s
correctness → instrument → profile → accelerate discipline, that
escalation is **not** taken now: this is one dataset, on the wrong camera,
and the acceptance gate above has not been satisfied.

# Experiment 2 — `feature_trackability`

## Success Criteria (per `docs/modules/EXPERIMENTAL-CV.md`)

- **Hypothesis/question:** Does ordinary, undirected head-mounted motion
  produce frame pairs with enough shared, geometrically-verified
  structure to make any multi-view geometry worth attempting at all?
- **Dataset/session:** same 150 sampled frames.
- **Metrics:** ORB keypoints/frame; ratio-test match count; RANSAC
  fundamental-matrix-verified inlier count and ratio; homography inlier
  count (degeneracy check); per-pair cost. Measured at frame gaps
  k ∈ {1, 2, 5, 10}.
- **Baseline:** none needed — this is an existence question about the
  data, not a comparison between approaches.
- **Method note:** verification uses the **fundamental matrix and a
  homography cross-check, never `recoverPose`** — so this experiment
  requires **no camera intrinsics** and is unaffected by the intrinsics
  gap that blocks Experiment 3.

## Measured

ORB (`nfeatures=1000`): **950.83 keypoints/frame mean** (median 996, min
229, max 1000). Detection cost **5.91 ms/frame mean** (median 3.46 ms;
the 374.8 ms max is first-call initialization, not steady state).

| Frame gap | Matches (mean) | Verified inliers (mean / median) | Inlier ratio (mean) | Homography inliers (mean) | Pairs ≥30 inliers | Match cost (mean) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **k=1** | 431.0 | **386.5 / 384** | **0.885** | 331.7 | **100.0%** | 1.92 ms |
| k=2 | 303.6 | 261.3 / 252 | 0.836 | 214.5 | **100.0%** | 2.37 ms |
| k=5 | 152.3 | 123.0 / 97 | 0.758 | 89.5 | 72.41% | 4.29 ms |
| k=10 | 67.2 | 51.2 / 24 | 0.645 | 35.0 | 47.14% | 5.65 ms |

## Result

**Clear positive on the headline question, with one important structural
caveat.**

- **Trackable structure is abundant at the rates the platform actually
  runs.** At k=1, **100% of the 149 frame pairs** cleared 30
  RANSAC-verified inliers, averaging **386 inliers at an 0.885 inlier
  ratio**. The research doc's central worry — that casual wearable motion
  might be a fundamentally harder regime than the deliberate/robot-mounted
  motion in standard VO benchmarks, with matches collapsing under real
  head motion — **is not supported by this footage.** That is a
  meaningful de-risking of the whole World Builder direction.
- **Cost is comfortably within budget.** Detection (5.91 ms) plus
  matching (1.92 ms at k=1) is **~7.8 ms/frame**, well under the
  ~20–30 ms envelope the shipped depth experiment already occupies
  (`V0.9.1` report). Classical ORB is viable at frame rate on CPU alone —
  no GPU, no model download.
- **Shared structure decays fast with frame gap**, and the median decays
  faster than the mean (k=10: mean 51.2 but median 24, i.e. a minority of
  well-textured pairs is holding the mean up while the typical pair has
  already degraded). Any future keyframe policy should therefore key off
  measured match counts, not a fixed frame interval.
- **⚠ Caveat — probable small-baseline/rotational degeneracy at k=1.**
  Homography inliers (331.7) are **86% of** fundamental-matrix inliers
  (386.5) at k=1. When a homography explains nearly as much as the
  fundamental matrix, the pair is consistent with near-pure rotation or a
  dominant plane — geometrically **degenerate for triangulation** even
  though match counts look excellent. The ratio improves with gap (k=5:
  89.5/123.0 = 73%; k=10: 35.0/51.2 = 68%), i.e. **parallax grows with
  baseline, as expected.** This sets up a real tension for Experiment 3
  that this result should be read as flagging in advance: *consecutive
  frames match well but may carry too little parallax to triangulate,
  while wider-baseline pairs carry parallax but retain far fewer
  matches.* This does not block Experiment 3 — that remains blocked on
  intrinsics — but it predicts where its difficulty will actually lie.

## Conclusions

1. **Both experiments answered their question with measured numbers.**
   Depth flicker on real motion is confirmed and quantified; cheap
   causal smoothing helps materially but does not produce a settled
   estimate. Trackable structure under undirected head-mounted motion is
   abundant and cheap to extract at platform frame rates.
2. **The single most important new fact:** passive, undirected wearable
   motion does **not** appear to be the trackability dead-end the
   research pass flagged as its biggest open risk — on this footage.
3. **The single most important new caveat:** high homography-vs-
   fundamental inlier overlap at short baselines suggests triangulation
   from consecutive frames will be ill-conditioned. Parallax, not match
   count, is the likely binding constraint for World Builder geometry.
4. **Nothing here validates the platform's own camera.** See the validity
   scope box at the top. The acceptance gate stands.

## What was NOT done (and why)

- **Experiment 3 (`monocular_pose_feasibility`) — not started.** Blocked
  on DAT camera intrinsics, which exist nowhere in this repository and
  require either `search_dat_docs` (Mac-side session) or empirical
  calibration against a real device. Master Guide §16/§17 place it
  explicitly out of scope. No substitute intrinsics were invented — a
  guessed focal length would have produced a plausible-looking trajectory
  with no validity whatsoever.
- **Experiment 4 (`depth_scale_fusion`) — not started.** Depends on
  Experiment 3 or on physically-measured reference distances, neither
  available.
- **No smoothing stage was added to the shipped module.** Choosing a
  production smoothing strategy on dataset-only evidence would violate the
  acceptance gate. The measurement exists to inform that choice later, not
  to make it now.
- **No new model or acceleration dependency was adopted.** Consistent with
  `docs/superpowers/research/2026-08-20-gpu-nvidia-roadmap.md`: no measured
  trigger was produced by this work.
